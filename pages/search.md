It looks like you are asking about the basic concepts of Augmented Reality (AR), based on Chapter 5 of the table of contents you shared.
At its simplest, Augmented Reality is a technology that "augments" (adds to) your physical surroundings by overlaying digital information onto the real world. Unlike Virtual Reality (VR), which creates a completely fake environment, AR keeps you grounded in the real world but enhances it.
Here are the basic concepts broken down:
1. The Core Definition
AR is the integration of digital information with the user's environment in real-time. While VR replaces your vision, AR adds to it using:
Visuals: 3D models, text, or animations.
Audio: Directional sound.
Haptics: Vibrations or touch feedback.
2. The Three Requirements
For a technology to be considered true AR (according to the standard definition by Ronald Azuma), it must:
Combine real and virtual content.
Be interactive in real-time.
Be registered in 3D (the digital object must look like it is actually sitting on a table or floor, not just floating on the screen).
3. How AR Works (The Architecture)
To make AR happen, a device needs three main "senses":
Sensing (Cameras and Sensors): The device needs to "see" the world. It uses cameras and sensors (like accelerometers and gyroscopes) to understand where the floor, walls, and objects are.
Processing: The computer (in your phone or headset) calculates where to place the digital object so it stays in the right spot even if you move.
Projection (Display): The result is shown to you through a screen (like a smartphone) or transparent lenses (like smart glasses).
4. Common Examples
Social Media: Filters on Snapchat or Instagram that put dog ears on your face or change your eye color.
Gaming: Pokémon GO is the most famous example, where creatures appear on the sidewalk through your phone screen.
Utility: IKEA’s app that lets you see how a couch would look in your actual living room before you buy it.
AR vs. VR vs. MR (A Quick Comparison)
Technology	Environment	How you see it
Virtual Reality (VR)	Fully Digital	Totally immersive (you can't see the real room).
Augmented Reality (AR)	Real World + Digital Layer	Digital objects are "painted" on top of the real world.
Mixed Reality (MR)	Real World + Interactive Digital	Digital objects can interact with real objects (e.g., a digital ball bouncing off your real table).
